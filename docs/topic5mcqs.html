Topic 5 - MCQs Formative Assessment

Author

by Dr Camilo Calderon

How to use this quiz

This quiz is designed as a formative self-assessment tool, not as a test.

Its purpose is to help you: - check your understanding of the key concepts from Topic 5, - identify which types of thinking you find more difficult, and - guide your revision before the tutorial and future assessments.

What this quiz measures

Each question belongs to one of four categories:

  • Subject Knowledge (SK): definitions, concepts, and facts.
  • Analysis (AN): understanding relationships and economic mechanisms.
  • Problem Solving (PS): applying concepts to new situations.
  • Mathematical Thinking (MT): working with equations and quantitative relationships.

Your score will be broken down by category so you can see where you are strong and where you need more practice.

How to complete the quiz

  • You may use your notes and the lecture materials.
  • Take your time — the goal is understanding, not speed.
  • Use the Hints if you get stuck.
  • You may refresh and retry the quiz as many times as you like.

How to interpret your results

  • A low score is not a problem — it tells you what to focus on.
  • If you score low in:
    • SK: revise definitions and core concepts.
    • AN: revisit the logic of the models and examples.
    • PS: practise applying concepts to new cases.
    • MT: practise the mathematical calculations and equations.

About the leaderboard

The leaderboard is optional and anonymous. It exists only to make practice more engaging.
It has no effect on your grade and is not used for evaluation.

The only purpose of this quiz is to support your learning.

Analysis

AN1) If expected inflation rises while the nominal policy rate is unchanged, what happens to the real interest rate?
Hint Use the Fisher decomposition to separate nominal and real components.
AN2) Why does price stickiness generate a short-run output response to monetary policy?
Hint Compare flexible-price neutrality with rigid-price dynamics.
AN3) If the central bank raises rates more than one-for-one with inflation (Taylor principle), what is stabilised?
Hint Consider determinacy conditions in rational expectations models.
AN4) Why does a negative demand shock lower both output and inflation in the NK model?
Hint Link the output gap to real marginal cost in the Phillips curve.
AN5) If the central bank keeps rates too low relative to the natural rate, the output gap will likely be:
Hint Compare the policy rate with the equilibrium real rate.
AN6) At the ELB, why can forward guidance stimulate current demand?
Hint Expectations about future borrowing costs affect current spending decisions.
AN7) If firms expect lower future marginal costs, what happens to current inflation?
Hint The NKPC is forward-looking.
AN8) Why is the Taylor principle necessary for a unique equilibrium?
Hint Think about how expectations can generate multiple equilibria.
AN9) If fiscal policy persistently raises demand without monetary accommodation, what must happen to interest rates under a Taylor rule?
Hint Apply the rule mechanically to higher inflation/output gap.
AN10) Why does the NK model reject strict long-run money neutrality as a policy guide in the short run?
Hint Separate long-run neutrality from short-run non-neutrality.

Problem Solving

PS1) Inflation rises above target while the output gap is positive. According to a Taylor rule, the central bank should:
Hint Consider how the rule responds to deviations in both arguments.
PS2) The natural real rate is 1%, expected inflation is 2%, and the central bank sets the nominal rate at 2%. What is the real policy rate?
Hint Subtract expected inflation from the nominal rate.
PS3) A negative technology shock raises marginal cost. What happens immediately to inflation?
Hint Use the cost channel in the Phillips curve.
PS4) If the central bank credibly commits to keeping rates low for longer, what happens to long-term interest rates?
Hint Long-term rates reflect expected future short rates.
PS5) The economy is at the ELB and faces a negative demand shock. The central bank announces future overshooting of inflation. Why might this stimulate demand today?
Hint Think intertemporally about expected inflation.
PS6) If the real rate exceeds the natural rate, what happens to the output gap over time?
Hint Compare borrowing costs to equilibrium conditions.
PS7) A positive demand shock raises output above potential. What happens to inflation over time if policy does not respond strongly enough?
Hint Use the forward-looking Phillips curve and weak policy response.
PS8) If the central bank violates the Taylor principle, what risk arises?
Hint Consider determinacy under rational expectations.
PS9) A permanent increase in the natural real rate occurs, but the policy rate does not adjust. What happens to the output gap?
Hint Compare the stance of policy relative to equilibrium.
PS10) Why can credible commitment to a future inflation overshoot help escape a liquidity trap?
Hint Expectations of higher inflation reduce real borrowing costs today.
