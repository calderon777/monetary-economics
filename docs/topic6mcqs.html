Topic 6 - MCQs Formative Assessment

Author

by Dr Camilo Calderon

How to use this quiz

This quiz is designed as a formative self-assessment tool, not as a test.

Its purpose is to help you: - check your understanding of the key concepts from Topic 6, - identify which types of thinking you find more difficult, and - guide your revision before the tutorial and future assessments.

What this quiz measures

Each question belongs to one of four categories:

  • Subject Knowledge (SK): definitions, concepts, and facts.
  • Analysis (AN): understanding relationships and economic mechanisms.
  • Problem Solving (PS): applying concepts to new situations.
  • Mathematical Thinking (MT): working with equations and quantitative relationships.

Your score will be broken down by category so you can see where you are strong and where you need more practice.

How to complete the quiz

  • You may use your notes and the lecture materials.
  • Take your time — the goal is understanding, not speed.
  • Use the Hints if you get stuck.
  • You may refresh and retry the quiz as many times as you like.

How to interpret your results

  • A low score is not a problem — it tells you what to focus on.
  • If you score low in:
    • SK: revise definitions and core concepts.
    • AN: revisit the logic of the models and examples.
    • PS: practise applying concepts to new cases.
    • MT: practise the mathematical calculations and equations.

About the leaderboard

The leaderboard is optional and anonymous. It exists only to make practice more engaging.
It has no effect on your grade and is not used for evaluation.

The only purpose of this quiz is to support your learning.

Subject Knowledge

SK1) In open-economy macroeconomics, the nominal exchange rate is defined as:
Hint Distinguish between nominal currency prices and relative goods prices.
SK2) The real exchange rate measures:
Hint Adjust the nominal exchange rate for price levels.
SK3) Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) in its absolute form implies that:
Hint PPP eliminates systematic differences in price levels across countries.
SK4) Uncovered Interest Parity (UIP) links domestic and foreign interest rates to:
Hint Investors compare expected returns across currencies.
SK5) Under a flexible exchange rate regime, monetary policy primarily affects:
Hint Exchange rates adjust to equilibrate asset markets.
SK6) An appreciation of the domestic currency means:
Hint Focus on what happens to the purchasing power of the currency.
SK7) The Marshall-Lerner condition determines whether a depreciation will:
Hint Consider price elasticities of exports and imports.
SK8) In the Mundell-Fleming model with perfect capital mobility and fixed exchange rates, monetary policy is:
Hint Capital flows offset domestic monetary changes under a peg.
SK9) A risk premium in the UIP condition implies that investors require compensation for:
Hint Expected returns may differ due to perceived uncertainty.
SK10) The impossible trinity (trilemma) states that a country cannot simultaneously have:
Hint Only two of the three policy goals can be achieved at once.

Analysis

AN1) If domestic interest rates rise above foreign rates with no change in expected depreciation, what happens immediately under UIP?
Hint Capital flows toward higher expected returns.
AN2) Why does monetary expansion lead to depreciation under flexible exchange rates?
Hint Track the interest rate channel through asset markets.
AN3) If PPP holds in the long run but not the short run, what explains temporary deviations?
Hint Goods prices may adjust more slowly than exchange rates.
AN4) Under fixed exchange rates with perfect capital mobility, why does fiscal expansion raise output?
Hint Consider how defending the peg affects the money supply.
AN5) If the Marshall-Lerner condition is not satisfied, a depreciation will:
Hint Think about inelastic trade volumes.
AN6) Why does capital mobility reduce monetary autonomy under a fixed exchange rate?
Hint Interest parity constrains domestic rates.
AN7) If investors expect future depreciation, what must happen to domestic interest rates under UIP?
Hint Compensation is required for expected currency loss.
AN8) Why can exchange rate overshooting occur after a monetary shock?
Hint Asset markets adjust instantly; goods markets adjust slowly.
AN9) If a country tries to fix its exchange rate while keeping capital mobility and independent monetary policy, what outcome emerges?
Hint Violating the trilemma creates pressure on reserves.
AN10) Why does a credible fixed exchange rate reduce inflation expectations?
Hint Credibility transmits the anchor country’s monetary stance.

Problem Solving

PS1) Domestic interest rates fall below foreign rates. Investors expect no exchange rate change. What happens to the domestic currency?
Hint Investors move funds toward higher returns.
PS2) The nominal exchange rate is 2 (domestic per foreign), domestic prices are 100, foreign prices are 200. What is the real exchange rate?
Hint Use \(q = \frac{EP^*}{P}\).
PS3) A country with flexible exchange rates increases money supply. Output rises in the short run. What happens to the exchange rate?
Hint Follow the interest rate channel.
PS4) If export and import elasticities sum to 0.8, what does the Marshall-Lerner condition imply?
Hint Compare the sum to unity.
PS5) Under fixed exchange rates and high capital mobility, the central bank cuts rates. What happens?
Hint Defending the peg requires reserve intervention.
PS6) Foreign interest rates rise. With perfect capital mobility and a fixed exchange rate, domestic rates must:
Hint Interest parity must hold under a credible peg.
PS7) Expected future depreciation increases by 3%. If foreign rates are unchanged, what must happen to domestic rates?
Hint Use the UIP condition algebraically.
PS8) A country attempts expansionary monetary policy under fixed exchange rates and perfect capital mobility. What is the long-run effect on output?
Hint Monetary policy loses autonomy under a peg.
PS9) A credible announcement of future monetary tightening causes the exchange rate today to:
Hint Exchange rates are forward-looking asset prices.
PS10) Why might a depreciation initially worsen the trade balance before improving it?
Hint Quantities adjust more slowly than prices.

Hint Higher relative inflation weakens the currency.

Hint Rearrange UIP and interpret the sign.
