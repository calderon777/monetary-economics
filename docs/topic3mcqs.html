EC3014 - Topic 3 MCQ Bank

Author

by Dr Camilo Calderon

How to use this quiz

This quiz is designed as a formative self-assessment tool, not as a test.

Its purpose is to help you: - check your understanding of the key concepts from Topic 3, - identify which types of thinking you find more difficult, and - guide your revision before the tutorial and future assessments.

What this quiz measures

Each question belongs to one of four categories:

  • Subject Knowledge (SK): definitions, concepts, and facts.
  • Analysis (AN): understanding relationships and economic mechanisms.
  • Problem Solving (PS): applying concepts to new situations.
  • Mathematical Thinking (MT): working with ratios, prices, and scaling.

Your score will be broken down by category so you can see where you are strong and where you need more practice.

How to complete the quiz

  • You may use your notes and the lecture materials.
  • Take your time — the goal is understanding, not speed.
  • Use the Hints if you get stuck.
  • You may refresh and retry the quiz as many times as you like.

How to interpret your results

  • A low score is not a problem — it tells you what to focus on.
  • If you score low in:
    • SK: revise definitions and core concepts.
    • AN: revisit the logic of the models and examples.
    • PS: practise applying concepts to new cases.
    • MT: practise the price and ratio calculations.

About the leaderboard

The leaderboard is optional and anonymous. It exists only to make practice more engaging.
It has no effect on your grade and is not used for evaluation.

The only purpose of this quiz is to support your learning.

Subject Knowledge

SK1) What does the Fisher equation relate?
Hint Recall the identity that decomposes nominal returns into real returns and expected inflation.
SK2) In the quantity theory of money, velocity refers to:
Hint Think about how frequently a unit of currency is used in transactions.
SK3) If money is neutral in the long run, a permanent increase in money growth affects:
Hint Distinguish between nominal and real variables when considering long-run neutrality.
SK4) Expected inflation primarily affects:
Hint Use the relationship between expected inflation and nominal returns.
SK5) Superneutrality of money implies that a change in money growth affects:
Hint Superneutrality strengthens neutrality by extending it to growth rates.
SK6) In a Taylor rule framework, the central bank responds systematically to:
Hint Think of the two main gaps policymakers typically stabilise.
SK7) A rise in expected inflation, holding the real rate constant, will:
Hint Apply the Fisher equation mechanically.
SK8) If velocity is unstable, a constant money growth rule may:
Hint Recall the quantity equation and what happens if one component varies unpredictably.
SK9) In the long run under classical assumptions, inflation is primarily determined by:
Hint Consider the proportional relationship implied by the quantity theory.
SK10) If the central bank increases money growth permanently, the Fisher effect predicts that in the long run:
Hint Separate short-run adjustment from long-run equilibrium conditions.

Analysis

AN1) If expected inflation rises but the nominal rate does not adjust immediately, what happens to the real rate?
Hint Subtract expected inflation from the nominal rate.
AN2) If money growth increases permanently and velocity is constant, inflation will:
Hint Use the steady-state implication of the quantity equation.
AN3) Under a Taylor rule, if inflation exceeds target, the central bank should:
Hint Stabilisation policy leans against inflationary pressure.
AN4) If money is neutral, an anticipated monetary expansion will:
Hint Focus on long-run classical separation.
AN5) If velocity increases unexpectedly while money supply is constant, nominal GDP will:
Hint Apply MV = PY holding M fixed.
AN6) If a central bank strictly follows a money growth rule but velocity becomes volatile, inflation will:
Hint Think about which components drive nominal income.
AN7) An increase in expected inflation leads lenders to:
Hint Consider compensation for expected loss of purchasing power.
AN8) If monetary policy is credible and expected inflation falls, the nominal rate will:
Hint Use forward-looking expectations in the Fisher equation.
AN9) Under superneutrality, raising money growth from 2% to 5% permanently changes:
Hint Superneutrality isolates nominal growth effects.
AN10) If output is above potential under a Taylor rule, the central bank will likely:
Hint Policy reacts to positive output gaps by tightening.

Problem Solving

PS1) A country doubles its money supply while velocity and output remain constant. What happens to prices?
Hint Use proportional reasoning from MV = PY.
PS2) Expected inflation is 4% and the nominal interest rate is 6%. What is the real interest rate?
Hint Subtract expected inflation from nominal rate.
PS3) If the central bank raises rates when inflation rises, it is following:
Hint Identify systematic feedback from inflation deviations.
PS4) Money growth rises from 3% to 7%, and real growth is constant at 2%. Long-run inflation becomes approximately:
Hint Use inflation approximations: money growth minus real growth.
PS5) If expected inflation increases but nominal rates adjust slowly, borrowers initially:
Hint Compute real rate before full adjustment.
PS6) Velocity rises 5% while money supply is constant and output fixed. Inflation will be approximately:
Hint Treat velocity growth like money growth in MV = PY.
PS7) If money growth permanently increases but inflation expectations adjust immediately, real output will:
Hint Apply neutrality with rational expectations.
PS8) A credible disinflation reduces expected inflation by 3 percentage points. If the real rate is unchanged, nominal rates will:
Hint Use the Fisher relationship symmetrically.
PS9) If output exceeds potential and inflation is above target, a Taylor rule implies:
Hint Consider both inflation and output gaps.
PS10) If money growth exceeds real growth persistently, long-run inflation will:
Hint Rearrange the steady-state quantity equation.

Mathematical Thinking

MT1) If nominal rate is 5% and expected inflation is 2%, the real rate is:
Hint Real rate is approximately nominal minus expected inflation.
MT2) Money growth is 8%, real growth is 3%, velocity constant. Inflation is approximately:
Hint Use steady-state approximation.
MT3) If MV = PY and M grows 4%, V constant, Y grows 1%, inflation is approximately:
Hint Subtract real growth from money growth.
MT4) Nominal rate 9%, expected inflation 6%. Real rate:
Hint Apply Fisher equation directly.
MT5) Money growth increases by 2 percentage points permanently. In the long run, inflation:
Hint Assume neutrality and constant real growth.
MT6) Velocity growth is 3%, money growth is 5%, real growth is 4%. Inflation is approximately:
Hint Combine money and velocity growth, then subtract real growth.
MT7) Expected inflation rises from 2% to 5%, real rate constant at 2%. Nominal rate becomes:
Hint Add real rate to new expected inflation.
MT8) Money growth 10%, velocity growth -2%, real growth 3%. Inflation is approximately:
Hint Net nominal growth equals money plus velocity growth.
MT9) If nominal rate is 12% and real rate is 4%, expected inflation is:
Hint Rearrange the Fisher equation.
MT10) Money growth equals real growth. Velocity constant. Inflation is:
Hint When nominal income and real output grow equally, prices are stable.
