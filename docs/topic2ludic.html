Create Your Own Song

Songs have long been used to communicate political, social, and economic ideas. In many contexts, including Latin American protest and revolutionary traditions (for example, forms of trova), music has helped people remember arguments, identities, and collective narratives. This activity asks a course-specific version of that question: why not songs about the Classicals, Keynesians, money demand, inflation, or monetary policy?

Welcome to the Song Generation Lab. In this activity, you will:

  1. Write lyrics or a short narrative using economic concepts from Topic 2
  2. Choose a music style (genre, mood, tempo, tone)
  3. Generate a song from your words and prompt
  4. Reflect on how well the output communicates the economics

Learning focus: The goal is not music production quality. The goal is to communicate monetary economics ideas clearly and creatively.

How You Will Be Assessed (Economics X-Factor Rubric)

Your song will be judged like a performance, but the judges are scoring economics learning.

Each criterion is scored from 1 to 10.

Criterion What judges look for 1-3 4-6 7-8 9-10
Economic Correctness Accuracy of claims about Topic 2 ideas Mostly inaccurate Some correct ideas, mixed with errors Mostly accurate Accurate, precise, no major errors
Concept Use Use of key concepts (e.g., inflation, velocity, money demand, quantity theory) Namedrops only or absent Uses some concepts but shallowly Concepts used meaningfully Concepts integrated fluently and insightfully
Causal Reasoning Clear cause-effect logic in the economics No clear causal links Some causal links, but confused or incomplete Clear causal links with minor gaps Strong, coherent causal chain with conditions/nuance
Communication Quality Clarity, memorability, and intelligibility of the economics message Hard to follow Partly clear Clear and engaging Clear, memorable, and teaches effectively
Revision Quality Improvement from first draft to revised draft (economics, not only style) Little/no improvement Some improvement Clear improvement in accuracy/clarity Major improvement with thoughtful corrections

Total score: /50

Your Creative Input

Use this workflow:

  1. Write 4-8 lines about one idea from Topic 2 (for example: inflation, money demand, velocity, quantity theory).
  2. Keep the economics accurate, even if the style is playful.
  3. Choose a music prompt that matches the tone of your message.
  4. Generate, listen, and revise your text if the meaning is unclear.

Submission Checklist (What You Must Hand In)

To make the rubric fair, submit:

  1. First draft lyrics/narrative (before listening to the generated result)
  2. Music prompt (genre/mood/style)
  3. One or more improved re-submissions under the same nickname (after listening and reflecting)
  4. Final version submitted for scoring

Why no separate revision box? For the pilot, improvement will be tracked by comparing submissions over time for the same nickname.

Reflection Questions

After listening to your generated song, consider:

  1. Authorship & Creativity: How did you and the AI contribute to this song? Who is the “author”?

  2. Economic Concepts: Does the music reflect the economic themes in your lyrics? Why or why not?

  3. Limitations: What did the AI do well? What would you change or improve?

  4. Monetary Economics Connection: How could this creative approach help explain monetary policy concepts to others?

  5. Ethical Reflection: In what ways is this different from a student writing a song entirely on their own?

  6. AI Risks (Optional): What are the risks of using AI-generated media in education (e.g., accuracy, bias, over-reliance, authorship confusion)?

Student Notes

  • If song generation is not working, tell your instructor (it may be a connection/server issue rather than your prompt).
  • You can still complete the economics task by drafting lyrics, explaining your intended style, and answering the reflection questions.
  • Revise your lyrics after listening: clearer economic language usually improves the educational value of the result.

Instructor/Technical Note (Optional)

This page connects to a music-generation backend through the ACE-Step API URL field. For student use, the technical setup should usually be handled by the instructor or institution so learners can focus on the economics task.
