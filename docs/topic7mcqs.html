Topic 7 - MCQs Formative Assessment

Author

by Dr Camilo Calderon

How to use this quiz

This quiz is designed as a formative self-assessment tool, not as a test.

Its purpose is to help you: - check your understanding of the key concepts from Topic 7, - identify which types of thinking you find more difficult, and - guide your revision before the tutorial and future assessments.

What this quiz measures

Each question belongs to one of four categories:

  • Subject Knowledge (SK): definitions, concepts, and facts.
  • Analysis (AN): understanding relationships and economic mechanisms.
  • Problem Solving (PS): applying concepts to new situations.
  • Mathematical Thinking (MT): working with equations and quantitative relationships.

Your score will be broken down by category so you can see where you are strong and where you need more practice.

How to complete the quiz

  • You may use your notes and the lecture materials.
  • Take your time — the goal is understanding, not speed.
  • Use the Hints if you get stuck.
  • You may refresh and retry the quiz as many times as you like.

How to interpret your results

  • A low score is not a problem — it tells you what to focus on.
  • If you score low in:
    • SK: revise definitions and core concepts.
    • AN: revisit the logic of the models and examples.
    • PS: practise applying concepts to new cases.
    • MT: practise the mathematical calculations and equations.

About the leaderboard

The leaderboard is optional and anonymous. It exists only to make practice more engaging.
It has no effect on your grade and is not used for evaluation.

The only purpose of this quiz is to support your learning.

Analysis

AN1) Why can maturity transformation make banks vulnerable to runs?
Hint Compare withdrawal timing with asset liquidity.
AN2) If deposit insurance is credible, the probability of a bank run decreases because:
Hint Expectations shape withdrawal incentives.
AN3) Why might high leverage amplify financial crises?
Hint Losses represent a larger fraction of capital when leverage is high.
AN4) If asset values fall sharply, what happens first on a bank’s balance sheet?
Hint Apply the accounting identity.
AN5) Why can deposit insurance create moral hazard?
Hint Risk monitoring depends on exposure to losses.
AN6) If a central bank lends freely at a penalty rate during a crisis, what principle is it following?
Hint Provide liquidity but discourage excessive risk.
AN7) Why can interconnectedness between banks transmit shocks?
Hint Think about interbank exposures.
AN8) If capital requirements increase, banks may reduce lending in the short run because:
Hint Adjust either the numerator (equity) or denominator (assets).
AN9) Why can a liquidity crisis become a solvency crisis?
Hint Forced sales can generate balance sheet losses.
AN10) Why is distinguishing illiquidity from insolvency crucial for policy?
Hint Liquidity support is appropriate only for solvent institutions.

Problem Solving

PS1) A bank holds long-term mortgages funded by demand deposits. Rumours spread about losses. Why is it vulnerable to a run?
Hint Timing mismatch creates vulnerability.
PS2) A bank has assets of 100 and liabilities of 90. What is equity?
Hint Use Assets = Liabilities + Equity.
PS3) If deposit insurance is removed suddenly, what is likely to happen to depositor behaviour?
Hint Incentives change when losses are possible.
PS4) A bank suffers loan losses of 8 when equity is 10. What remains of equity?
Hint Losses reduce equity directly.
PS5) During a crisis, solvent banks cannot roll over short-term funding. What should the central bank do under lender-of-last-resort principles?
Hint Separate liquidity from solvency.
PS6) If required capital ratio is 10% and assets equal 200, minimum equity must be:
Hint Multiply assets by the required ratio.
PS7) A bank increases leverage while asset risk rises. What happens to systemic risk?
Hint Thin capital buffers amplify shocks.
PS8) If multiple banks hold similar assets and prices fall sharply, what macroeconomic effect may follow?
Hint Balance sheet repair reduces lending.
PS9) A credible guarantee eliminates panic but encourages future risk-taking. What trade-off is illustrated?
Hint Insurance reduces runs but alters incentives.
PS10) Why might higher equity requirements reduce long-run crisis probability?
Hint Focus on loss-absorption capacity.
