TutorialX

AI-supported tutorial questions for large-cohort economics teaching

Author

Dr Camilo Calderon

Published

June 1, 2026

Purpose

TutorialX is a reusable tutorial layer built around teacher-authored question sets.

  • Starts from topicXquestions.qmd-style material
  • Converts tutorial questions into structured, student-facing practice
  • Adds formative AI feedback without removing the human tutor
  • Scales tutorial preparation and follow-up for very large cohorts

Why This Matters

Large economics modules face a familiar tutorial problem.

  • Students arrive with uneven preparation
  • Tutorial time is scarce and often consumed by basic clarification
  • Feedback demand exceeds staff capacity
  • Weak students need earlier diagnosis, not only end-of-term intervention
  • Strong students need harder prompts and extensions

Pedagogical Model

TutorialX is designed as a structured active-learning cycle.

  1. Students attempt the tutorial question before or after class
  2. The system gives formative hints, not final answers
  3. Students revise their reasoning
  4. Tutors use patterns of difficulty to target tutorial time
  5. Post-tutorial feedback reinforces the main learning points

What Students Practise

The tool supports discipline-specific abilities rather than generic chatbot use.

  • Conceptual precision: definitions, assumptions, mechanisms
  • Economic reasoning: causal chains and comparative statics
  • Mathematical fluency: setting up equations and interpreting results
  • Writing: concise explanation under academic standards
  • Metacognition: comparing own answer with indicative reasoning

Tutorial Workflow

TutorialX can sit before, during, and after the live tutorial.

  • Before: students submit draft answers and receive low-stakes hints
  • During: tutors focus on misconceptions visible from aggregate patterns
  • After: students receive recap prompts, extensions, and revision guidance
  • Between weeks: the system remains available for self-paced practice

Tracking Learning

The useful data are pedagogical, not surveillance-oriented.

  • Question completion and revision attempts
  • Common missing concepts or incorrect mechanisms
  • Distribution of confidence and answer quality
  • Topics generating repeated confusion
  • Requests for hints, examples, or mathematical setup

Feedback After Tutorials

TutorialX can turn tutorial sessions into a feedback loop.

  • Tutors review anonymised pain points after each tutorial
  • AI summarises recurring errors for staff review
  • Students receive targeted post-tutorial prompts
  • Course team identifies questions that need rewriting
  • Repeated difficulties inform lecture recaps and office hours

On-Demand Student Use

Students can use TutorialX whenever they need practice.

  • No need to wait for office hours for first-stage feedback
  • Students can safely test partial answers
  • International and commuting students get more flexible support
  • Revision becomes more dialogic than reading static model answers
  • Indicative answers remain available for self-checking, not copying

Role of AI

AI is the formative layer.

  • Gives hints and diagnostic comments
  • Asks students to complete missing steps
  • Explains why an answer is incomplete
  • Adapts examples to the student’s current response
  • Flags uncertainty where human review is needed

AI should not be positioned as the authority of record.

Role of the Human Tutor

The human tutor remains academically central.

  • Designs the questions and indicative answers
  • Sets the standard of disciplinary reasoning
  • Interprets ambiguous or original student responses
  • Handles pastoral, ethical, and assessment concerns
  • Turns aggregate patterns into better teaching decisions

TutorialX expands the tutor’s reach; it does not replace tutorial judgement.

Tool Features

The current implementation pattern already supports the core feature set.

  • Teacher mode: create, edit, and save tutorials as JSON
  • Student mode: answer questions, request AI feedback, reveal indicative answers
  • Markdown and LaTeX support for economics content
  • Downloadable student answers for revision records
  • Optional Hugging Face Dataset persistence
  • Model/provider can be changed without rewriting tutorial content

Live Prototype

TutorialX now runs as one reusable application that can preload any topic from the existing topicXquestions.qmd and topicXanswers.qmd source files.

Academic Governance

The design needs explicit boundaries.

  • Feedback should be formative and non-assessed unless validated
  • Prompts should avoid giving complete answers by default
  • Students should be told when AI feedback may be incomplete or wrong
  • Human review is required for high-stakes or contested claims
  • Usage analytics should be anonymised or minimised where possible

Cost Interpretation

The AI feedback cost is small relative to tutorial staffing costs.

  • Even a conservative V4 Pro cache-miss estimate is about $0.015 per student
  • V4 Flash cache-miss estimate is about $0.005 per student
  • Output tokens dominate cost, so concise feedback design matters
  • Staff review time, governance, and support design are the real costs

Implementation Path

A pragmatic rollout can be staged.

  1. Import existing topicXquestions.qmd and topicXanswers.qmd files into the reusable app
  2. Pilot with one week of tutorials
  3. Compare AI feedback against tutor expectations
  4. Add tracking dashboard for aggregate misconceptions
  5. Scale to other modules and partner institutions once governance and support routines are stable

Evaluation Questions

For colleagues, the key issue is not whether the tool is impressive.

  • Do students submit better tutorial answers after using it?
  • Does it reduce basic clarification time in tutorials?
  • Does it help weaker students ask better questions?
  • Does it improve feedback consistency across tutorial groups?
  • Does it preserve the intellectual role of the human tutor?

Closing Proposition

TutorialX is best understood as a tutorial support infrastructure.

It gives students more opportunities to practise, gives tutors better information before and after contact hours, and keeps academic judgement with the teaching team.
