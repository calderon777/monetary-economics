Topic 4 - MCQs Formative Assessment

Author

by Dr Camilo Calderon

How to use this quiz

This quiz is designed as a formative self-assessment tool, not as a test.

Its purpose is to help you: - check your understanding of the key concepts from Topic 4, - identify which types of thinking you find more difficult, and - guide your revision before the tutorial and future assessments.

What this quiz measures

Each question belongs to one of four categories:

  • Subject Knowledge (SK): definitions, concepts, and facts.
  • Analysis (AN): understanding relationships and economic mechanisms.
  • Problem Solving (PS): applying concepts to new situations.
  • Mathematical Thinking (MT): working with equations and quantitative relationships.

Your score will be broken down by category so you can see where you are strong and where you need more practice.

How to complete the quiz

  • You may use your notes and the lecture materials.
  • Take your time — the goal is understanding, not speed.
  • Use the Hints if you get stuck.
  • You may refresh and retry the quiz as many times as you like.

How to interpret your results

  • A low score is not a problem — it tells you what to focus on.
  • If you score low in:
    • SK: revise definitions and core concepts.
    • AN: revisit the logic of the models and examples.
    • PS: practise applying concepts to new cases.
    • MT: practise the mathematical calculations and equations.

About the leaderboard

The leaderboard is optional and anonymous. It exists only to make practice more engaging.
It has no effect on your grade and is not used for evaluation.

The only purpose of this quiz is to support your learning.

Subject Knowledge

SK1) In the quantity theory identity (\(MV = PY\)), what does \(V\) represent?
Hint Recall that the identity links money to nominal spending through how frequently money circulates.
SK2) According to the monetarist long-run benchmark, a permanent increase in the level of money primarily affects:
Hint Apply the principle of long-run neutrality in the classical dichotomy.
SK3) In the canonical monetarist model, equilibrium employment (\(N^*\)) is determined in the:
Hint Identify which block determines real wages and employment before nominal variables are considered.
SK4) The Fisher equation links the nominal interest rate (\(i\)) to:
Hint Distinguish between real returns and inflation compensation.
SK5) Money is described as “neutral” in the long run because changes in money:
Hint Use the classical dichotomy to separate real and nominal variables.
SK6) Under stable velocity and constant real output growth, a higher permanent money growth rate implies:
Hint Start from \(MV=PY\) and consider growth rates.
SK7) The natural-rate hypothesis implies that attempts to keep unemployment below \(u^*\):
Hint Think about how expectations adjust in the Phillips curve framework.
SK8) Hyperinflation, under the Cagan criterion, begins when monthly inflation exceeds:
Hint Recall the numerical threshold used in historical classifications.
SK9) In the Cagan money demand specification, higher expected inflation leads to:
Hint Consider how inflation expectations affect the opportunity cost of holding money.
SK10) The constant money-growth (\(k\%\)) rule is motivated primarily by concerns about:
Hint Focus on the critique of discretionary fine-tuning.

Analysis

AN1) If velocity becomes unstable due to financial innovation, which monetarist proposition is most directly weakened?
Hint Identify which empirical assumption connects money growth predictably to spending.
AN2) Suppose prices adjust fully but nominal wages remain fixed temporarily after a monetary expansion. What happens to the real wage (\(w/P\))?
Hint Compare the speed of adjustment between numerator and denominator.
AN3) Why does the expectations-augmented Phillips curve imply no permanent inflation-unemployment trade-off?
Hint Consider how adaptive expectations change the position of the short-run curve.
AN4) If expected inflation rises permanently while the real interest rate is unchanged in the long run, what must happen to the nominal interest rate?
Hint Apply the Fisher relation carefully.
AN5) A central bank increases money growth, but velocity simultaneously falls sharply during a crisis. What is the most likely short-run effect on inflation?
Hint Use the identity \(MV=PY\) and allow both components to change.
AN6) In the monetarist four-block model, which adjustment restores long-run equilibrium after a monetary shock?
Hint Distinguish between real equilibrium determination and nominal adjustment.
AN7) Why might money-growth targeting become impractical even if long-run neutrality holds?
Hint Separate theoretical neutrality from empirical stability of money demand.
AN8) At the effective lower bound, why can QE still reduce longer-term yields?
Hint Think in terms of portfolio-balance mechanisms.
AN9) In a high-inflation environment, raising inflation further may generate less seigniorage revenue because:
Hint Consider how the inflation tax base responds to higher expected inflation.
AN10) Why does the natural-rate hypothesis undermine activist stabilization aimed at permanently lowering unemployment?
Hint Focus on dynamic expectation adjustment rather than static correlations.

Problem Solving

PS1) A country increases its money supply permanently. In the short run, workers suffer from money illusion and underestimate inflation. What is the most likely temporary outcome?
Hint Consider how misperceptions affect labour demand decisions.
PS2) Suppose money growth rises from 4% to 8% while real output growth remains at 2% and velocity is stable. What happens to long-run inflation?
Hint Use the growth-rate version of the quantity equation.
PS3) During a crisis, the central bank doubles money growth, but households sharply increase liquidity preference. Inflation remains low. Which explanation fits monetarist logic?
Hint Separate money supply changes from spending behaviour.
PS4) Expected inflation increases by 3 percentage points. If the real interest rate is unchanged, what happens to the nominal rate?
Hint Apply the Fisher equation directly.
PS5) A government finances persistent deficits through money creation. Expected inflation rises sharply, and real money balances collapse. What risk emerges?
Hint Consider nonlinear effects in money demand.
PS6) If unemployment is pushed below \(u^*\) through expansionary policy, what must occur over time to restore equilibrium?
Hint Trace the implications of adaptive expectations.
PS7) A permanent increase in money growth raises inflation expectations immediately. What happens to real output in the long run?
Hint Recall super-neutrality in the benchmark model.
PS8) If velocity falls persistently while money growth is constant, what must happen to inflation in the long run (holding real growth constant)?
Hint Work with growth rates in the quantity identity.
PS9) A credible fiscal reform eliminates expected future monetisation of deficits. What is the likely immediate effect on expected inflation?
Hint Expectations respond to regime credibility.
PS10) Why might a central bank prefer a rule over discretion in the presence of long and variable lags?
Hint Focus on informational limits and transmission delays.

Mathematical Thinking

MT1) Given \(MV = PY\), if \(M=100\), \(V=5\), and \(Y=250\), what is \(P\)?
Hint Rearrange the identity to isolate the price level.
MT2) If money growth \(\mu = 6\%\) and real output growth \(g = 2\%\), with stable velocity, inflation \(\pi\) is approximately:
Hint Use \(\pi \approx \mu - g\).
MT3) From \(P = \frac{M}{kY^s}\), if \(M\) doubles while \(k\) and \(Y^s\) are constant, \(P\):
Hint Identify proportional relationships.
MT4) Using \(i \approx r + \pi^e\), if \(r=2\%\) and \(\pi^e=5\%\), then \(i\) equals:
Hint Add real return and expected inflation.
MT5) In \(\pi_t = \pi_t^e - \alpha(u_t - u^*)\), if \(u_t = u^*\), then inflation equals:
Hint Substitute carefully when the unemployment gap is zero.
MT6) If real money demand follows \(\frac{M}{P} = A e^{-\alpha \pi^e}\), what happens to \(\frac{M}{P}\) when \(\pi^e\) rises?
Hint Consider the sign in the exponent.
MT7) If \(\mu = 10\%\), \(g=3\%\), and velocity growth is 2%, inflation equals approximately:
Hint Use growth accounting: \(\mu + v = \pi + g\).
MT8) If \(u^* = 5\%\), \(u_t=3\%\), \(\alpha=0.5\), and \(\pi_t^e=4\%\), what is \(\pi_t\) (ignoring shocks)?
Hint Compute the unemployment gap first.
MT9) If seigniorage revenue is \(S = \pi \cdot \frac{M}{P}\) and real balances fall as inflation rises, the relationship between \(S\) and \(\pi\) is most likely:
Hint Combine the inflation tax rate with a shrinking tax base.
MT10) If expected inflation rises by 4 percentage points and nominal rates rise by 4 percentage points while real rates remain unchanged, this empirical pattern supports:
Hint Look for one-for-one adjustment between inflation expectations and nominal rates.
